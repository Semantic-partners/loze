# Loze

An upper-level ontology largely inspired - with a few deviations and additions - by the Four-Category Ontology outlined by the philosopher E. J. Lowe (2006), supplemented with the concept of a schematic particular explored by Eddy Zemach (1970).

One salient feature of Loze is the category of *eventualities*, which correspond to what Lowe calls *modes* (2006). This top-level class encompasses entities that in other ontologies are variously called 'events', 'qualities', 'activities', 'relations' etc... . While those are traditionally considered to be disjoint categories, we believe that the criteria used for distinguishing between them are either superficial or impractical. With this in mind, our proposal is to merge them in one comprehensive category that will be used whenever we need to talk of the way in which something is or goes; for specifying the roles , we outlined an apparatus of thematic relations based on decades of research in philosophy and linguistics (). 

The category of schematic particulars is the other distinctive feature of Loze: such entities are repeatable like classes, but differently from classes they share the essential properties of their concretizations. While the very concept might come across as weird to those who are used to the class-individual duality, it has a robust philosophical underpinning, and we believe that it can provide surprisingly powerful design patterns, which can vastly simplify modelling taks that are usually addressed using OWL restrictions or punning.

**References**

Lowe, E. J. (2006). *The Four-Category Ontology*, Oxford: Clarendon Press.

Zemach, E. M. (1970). "Four Ontologies", *The Journal of Philosophy*, 67 (8): 231-47.
