# loze

An upper-level ontology largely inspired - with a few deviations and additions - by the Four-Category Ontology outlined by the philosopher E. J. Lowe (2006), supplemented with the concept of a schematic particular explored by Eddy Zemach (1970).

The category of schematic particulars is the most peculiar feature of Loze: those entities are repeatable like classes, but differently from classes they share the essential properties of their concretizations. While the concept of a schematic particular might come across as weird to those who are used to the class-individual duality, it has a robust philosophical underpinning, and we believe that it can provide surprisingly powerful design patterns, which can vastly simplify modelling taks that are usually addressed using OWL restrictions or punning.

**References**

Lowe, E. J. (2006). *The Four-Category Ontology*, Oxford: Clarendon Press.

Zemach, E. M. (1970). "Four Ontologies", *The Journal of Philosophy*, 67 (8): 231-47.
