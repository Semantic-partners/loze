# Loze

Loze is an upper-level ontology largely inspired by the Four-Category Ontology outlined by the philosopher Jonathan Lowe (2006, 2009), supplemented with the concept of a schematic particular investigated by Eddy Zemach (1970, 1992).

One distinctive feature of Loze is the category of *eventualities*, which correspond to what Lowe calls *modes*. This top-level class encompasses entities that in other ontologies are variously called 'events', 'processes', 'qualities', 'activities', 'relations' etc... . While those are usually considered to be disjoint categories, we believe that the criteria used for distinguishing between them are philosophically superficial and impractical. With this in mind, we propose to merge them into one comprehensive category that can be used whenever we need to model the way in which something is or goes at a given period of time. For specifying the roles that entities occupy in an eventuality, we propose an apparatus of thematic relations that draws from decades of research at the interface of philosophy and linguistics (Parsons 1990 & 1995; Orilia 2019). We believe that the result is a highly versatile and scalable pattern that can be easily applied across domains.

The category of schematic particulars is inspired by what Zemach calls *types*. Such entities are repeatable like classes, but differently from classes they share the essential properties of their concretizations. While the very concept might come across as weird to those who are used to the Semantic Web class-individual duality, it has a robust philosophical underpinning, and we believe that it can provide surprisingly powerful design patterns, which can vastly simplify modelling tasks that are usually addressed using OWL restrictions or punning.
