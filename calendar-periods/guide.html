A suggested extension, not core loze. Nothing in the foundation or the other modules depends on it. Use it as it is, change it, or take only the idea.

Most data is dated by the day, and most questions about it are asked by the quarter, the financial year or the season. This extension gives those periods names, and a script generates them for a range of dates, each linked to the periods it falls in. A query for "everything in FY2026 Q3" is then a property path, with no date arithmetic in the query.

Files

  • calendar-periods/generate_periods.py — the generator. Python 3, standard library plus PyYAML. Same input, same bytes.
  • calendar-periods/example.yaml — an example configuration with two organisations.
  • calendar-periods/sample-payments.ttl — sample payments dated within the example's range.
  • calendar-periods/test_generate_periods.py — the tests: python3 -m unittest discover -s calendar-periods.
pip install pyyaml
python3 calendar-periods/generate_periods.py calendar-periods/example.yaml -o periods.ttl

Configuration

base: https://example.org/time/       # generated IRIs start here
range:                                # days are generated for exactly this range
  start: 2025-04-01
  end: 2026-03-31
granularity: [day, month, quarter, year, fiscal_quarter, fiscal_year, season]  # add week for ISO weeks
orgs:                                 # or a single org: mapping
  - id: acme-ltd                      # fiscal IRIs are namespaced by this
    label: Acme Ltd
    iri: https://example.org/acme-ltd # optional; default <base>org/<id>
    fiscal_year_start: {month: 4, day: 1}
    label_by: end                     # FY2026 = 1 Apr 2025 to 31 Mar 2026; start would call it FY2025
    seasons: {scheme: meteorological, hemisphere: north}   # or astronomical; north or south

Any month, quarter, year, fiscal period or season that overlaps the range is generated whole, with its true start and end. A fiscal year can start on day 1 to 28 of any month.

IRI patterns

Relative to base. Every pattern is readable and fixed by the date, so two runs over overlapping ranges agree on the names they share.

A season is named by the year it starts in, so December 2025 to February 2026 is 2025-winter in the north; its label says "winter 2025-26".

Membership

Every period is a loze:PeriodOfTime with loze:hasStartDate and loze:hasEndDate, both xsd:date and both inclusive. Membership is loze:isSubperiodOf, which is transitive.

  • Calendar: day → month → quarter → year. Days also link to their ISO week.
  • Fiscal: fiscal quarter → fiscal year.
  • Between the two, and to seasons: a calendar period links to the smallest fiscal period, or season, that contains it, unless the next calendar period up already links there. So each link is written once, from the largest calendar period that fits.

With an April fiscal year, quarter/2025-Q4 links to FY2026-Q3 and the months and days under it reach it through the quarter. With meteorological seasons a month links to its season; with astronomical seasons the days at either end of a season link to it directly. If a fiscal year starts mid-month, its quarters split months, and days link to them directly. A level left out of granularity is skipped: without months, days link to quarters.

<day/2025-11-16> a loze:CalendarDay ;
    loze:hasStartDate "2025-11-16"^^xsd:date ;
    loze:hasEndDate   "2025-11-16"^^xsd:date ;
    loze:isSubperiodOf <month/2025-11> .
<month/2025-11> loze:isSubperiodOf <quarter/2025-Q4> ,
    <season/meteorological-north/2025-autumn> .
<quarter/2025-Q4> loze:isSubperiodOf <year/2025> , <fiscal/acme-ltd/FY2026-Q3> .
<fiscal/acme-ltd/FY2026-Q3> a loze:FiscalQuarter ;
    loze:hasStartDate  "2025-10-01"^^xsd:date ;
    loze:hasEndDate    "2025-12-31"^^xsd:date ;
    loze:isSubperiodOf <fiscal/acme-ltd/FY2026> ;
    loze:isDelimitedBy <fiscal/acme-ltd/calendar> .

Without a reasoner, query with loze:isSubperiodOf+ or *. With one, owl:TransitiveProperty fills in the rest.

Calendar 2025 Q4 and Acme's FY2026 Q3 cover the same days. Loze individuates a period by where it sits on the time axis, so strictly they are one period under two names. The generator keeps both names and links the calendar one to the fiscal one, so neither organisation's name leaks onto the other's.

The conventions are data too

Each organisation gets a loze:FiscalCalendar, and each season scheme a loze:SeasonScheme. Every fiscal period and season says which one delimits it. A graph can then hold the calendars of several organisations at once, and a query can ask which convention named a period.

<https://example.org/acme-ltd> a loze:Organisation ;
    loze:hasFiscalCalendar <fiscal/acme-ltd/calendar> .
<fiscal/acme-ltd/calendar> a loze:FiscalCalendar ;
    loze:isFiscalCalendarOf <https://example.org/acme-ltd> ;
    loze:hasFiscalYearStart "--04-01"^^xsd:gMonthDay ;
    loze:namesFiscalYearBy  "end" ;
    loze:usesSeasonScheme   <season/meteorological-north> .
<season/meteorological-north> a loze:SeasonScheme ;
    loze:hasSeasonMethod "meteorological" ;
    loze:hasHemisphere   "north" .

On the same day, 16 November 2025, Acme (April start, named by the end) is in FY2026 Q3 and Supplier Ltd (July start, named by the start) is in FY2025 Q2.

Seasons

Meteorological seasons are whole months: spring from 1 March, summer from 1 June, autumn from 1 September, winter from 1 December. Astronomical seasons use fixed dates for the equinoxes and solstices: 20 March, 21 June, 22 September and 21 December. That is an approximation. The real dates move by a day either way from year to year, so a day next to a boundary can land in the wrong season. The scheme says so in its own rdfs:comment. In the south the names swap: the dates of northern autumn are southern spring.

How it fits loze

Every new class is a subclass of loze:PeriodOfTime, and membership reuses loze:isSubperiodOf from the temporal relations module, so loze:atTime, loze:withinTime and the rest apply unchanged. The two conventions are information artefacts, and a fiscal calendar belongs to a loze:Organisation from the social extension. Like that extension, the terms sit in the loze namespace in a module of their own.

Loze does not import OWL-Time, and neither does this. The nearest OWL-Time terms are time:intervalDuring for loze:isSubperiodOf, and time:hasBeginning and time:hasEnd for the two date properties, though those point to an instant rather than to a date. Map them yourself if your data already uses OWL-Time.

Queries

Load periods.ttl from the command above and sample-payments.ttl.

Everything in Acme's FY2026 Q3. * rather than +, so something timed by the quarter itself is found too. The November retainer is timed by the month, and is found.

PREFIX loze: <https://loze-ontology.org/>
PREFIX rdfs: <http://www.w3.org/2000/01/rdf-schema#>

SELECT ?thing ?when WHERE {
  ?thing loze:atTime ?period .
  ?period loze:isSubperiodOf* <https://example.org/time/fiscal/acme-ltd/FY2026-Q3> ;
          rdfs:label ?when .
}

Which fiscal quarter is 16 November 2025 in, for each organisation, and by what convention.

PREFIX loze: <https://loze-ontology.org/>

SELECT ?org ?quarter ?fyStart ?namedBy WHERE {
  <https://example.org/time/day/2025-11-16> loze:isSubperiodOf+ ?quarter .
  ?quarter a loze:FiscalQuarter ;
           loze:isDelimitedBy ?calendar .
  ?calendar loze:isFiscalCalendarOf ?org ;
            loze:hasFiscalYearStart ?fyStart ;
            loze:namesFiscalYearBy ?namedBy .
}

Payments per northern meteorological season.

PREFIX loze: <https://loze-ontology.org/>
PREFIX rdfs: <http://www.w3.org/2000/01/rdf-schema#>

SELECT ?season (COUNT(?payment) AS ?n) WHERE {
  ?payment loze:atTime ?period .
  ?period loze:isSubperiodOf* ?s .
  ?s a loze:Season ;
     loze:isDelimitedBy <https://example.org/time/season/meteorological-north> ;
     rdfs:label ?season .
}
GROUP BY ?season

Payments per Acme fiscal quarter, reaching the organisation through its calendar rather than by IRI pattern.

PREFIX loze: <https://loze-ontology.org/>
PREFIX rdfs: <http://www.w3.org/2000/01/rdf-schema#>

SELECT ?quarter (COUNT(?payment) AS ?n) WHERE {
  ?payment loze:atTime ?period .
  ?period loze:isSubperiodOf* ?q .
  ?q a loze:FiscalQuarter ;
     loze:isDelimitedBy/loze:isFiscalCalendarOf <https://example.org/acme-ltd> ;
     rdfs:label ?quarter .
}
GROUP BY ?quarter
ORDER BY ?quarter
